Important energy distinction

1 · Foundations

The hallmark of simple harmonic motion

Periodic motion is not automatically SHM

Periodic motion means motion that repeats after a fixed interval of time.

Simple harmonic motion is a special type of periodic motion. The acceleration must be proportional to displacement from equilibrium and point in the opposite direction.

a = −ω²x Fundamental kinematic condition for SHM

For a mass attached to an ideal spring, Hooke's law gives

F = −kx Spring restoring force

Newton's second law gives

ma = −kx

Therefore

a = −(k/m)x

Comparing this with a = −ω²x gives

ω = √(k/m)

The minus sign is crucial. If the object is displaced to the right, the acceleration points left. If it is displaced to the left, the acceleration points right.

Amplitude A

Maximum magnitude of displacement from equilibrium.

Period T

Time required for one complete oscillation.

Frequency f

Number of complete oscillations per second. Unit: hertz (Hz).

Angular frequency ω

Rate at which the phase changes.

ω = 2πf = 2π/T

Phase

The quantity ωt + φ tells us where the oscillator is within its cycle.

Phase constant φ

Determines the object's starting position and direction of motion at t = 0.

4 · Energy in SHM

Kinetic and potential energy continually trade places

Move the oscillator by hand

Potential energy U = ½kx²
Kinetic energy K = ½k(A² − x²)
Total energy E = ½kA²
U / E
0%
K / E
100%

At equilibrium

x = 0 therefore U = 0 and K = E. The object is travelling fastest.

At the turning points

x = ±A therefore K = 0 and U = E. The object is instantaneously stationary.

Energy oscillates faster

Because U and K contain squared quantities, their values repeat twice during every displacement cycle.

5 · Initial conditions and phase

Convert x₀ and v₀ into amplitude and phase

The reliable method

At t = 0:

x₀ = A cos φ
v₀ = −ωA sin φ

Divide the velocity equation by ω, square both equations and add.

A = √[x₀² + (v₀/ω)²]

Then calculate phase using both signs:

φ = atan2(−v₀/ω, x₀)

Common mistake: Using only arccos(x₀/A) does not tell you whether the object is initially moving left or right.

6 · Pendulums and angular SHM

The same mathematics appears in rotational systems

Simple pendulum

A point mass m suspended from a light string of length L experiences a tangential restoring force

Ft = −mg sin θ

For sufficiently small angles measured in radians,

sin θ ≈ θ

The equation then becomes linear and the pendulum behaves approximately as SHM.

ω = √(g/L)
T = 2π√(L/g)

Notice that the period is independent of the bob's mass for the ideal small-angle model.

Physical pendulum

A rigid object may oscillate around a pivot. If h is the distance between the pivot and centre of mass and I is the rotational inertia about the pivot,

T = 2π√(I/mgh)

A simple pendulum is therefore only one special type of pendulum.

Torsion oscillator

If twisting produces a restoring torque

τ = −κθ

then rotational Newton's second law gives

ω = √(κ/I)

7 · Damping and forced oscillations

From ideal oscillators to real systems

Damped SHM

A common model for a damping force is

Fd = −bv

The equation of motion becomes

mx'' + bx' + kx = 0

For underdamped motion,

x = A₀e^(−bt/2m) cos(ωdt + φ)
ωd = √(k/m − b²/4m²)

The exponential term determines the shrinking amplitude envelope.

Because mechanical energy is proportional to amplitude squared,

E(t) = E₀e^(−bt/m)

Forced oscillations

Suppose an external force acts periodically:

F(t) = F₀ cos(ωt)

After the transient behaviour dies away, the oscillator moves at the driving frequency rather than necessarily at its natural frequency.

Resonance

The response becomes particularly large when the driving frequency is near the natural frequency.

ωdrive ≈ ω₀

Damping controls how sharp and high the resonance peak becomes.

Key idea: Resonance is fundamentally about efficient transfer of energy from a periodic driver into an oscillator.

8 · Worked examples

Learn a repeatable problem-solving method

1

Identify the oscillator and equilibrium position.

2

Write the governing SHM relation.

3

Translate the given information into variables.

4

Solve symbolically before inserting numbers.

5

Example 1 — Find T and f from m and k

A 0.50 kg block oscillates on a spring with spring constant 200 N/m. Find its angular frequency, period and frequency.

Step 1: identify the governing equation

ω = √(k/m)

Step 2: substitute

ω = √(200 / 0.50)

ω = √400

ω = 20 rad/s

Step 3: calculate period

T = 2π/ω

T = 2π/20

T = 0.314 s

Step 4: calculate frequency

f = 1/T

f = 3.18 Hz

ω = 20 rad/s, T = 0.314 s, f = 3.18 Hz
Example 2 — Maximum speed and acceleration

An oscillator has amplitude A = 0.12 m and frequency f = 2.5 Hz. Find its maximum speed and acceleration.

First find angular frequency:

ω = 2πf

ω = 2π(2.5)

ω = 15.71 rad/s

Maximum speed:

vmax = ωA

vmax = 15.71 × 0.12

vmax = 1.89 m/s

Maximum acceleration:

amax = ω²A

amax = (15.71)²(0.12)

amax ≈ 29.6 m/s²

Example 3 — Speed at a particular position

A spring oscillator has amplitude 0.20 m, spring constant 80 N/m and mass 0.50 kg. Find the speed when x = 0.12 m.

Use

v² = ω²(A² − x²)

Calculate ω:

ω = √(80/0.50)

ω = √160

ω ≈ 12.65 rad/s

Then

v = ω√(A² − x²)

v = 12.65√(0.20² − 0.12²)

v = 12.65√0.0256

|v| ≈ 2.02 m/s

There are two possible velocity directions at this position. Energy determines the speed but not the sign of velocity.

Example 4 — Energy of a spring oscillator

A spring with k = 50 N/m oscillates with amplitude 0.30 m. Find the total mechanical energy.

E = ½kA²

E = ½(50)(0.30²)

E = 25(0.09)

E = 2.25 J

Example 5 — Potential and kinetic energy

For the previous oscillator, find U and K when x = 0.18 m.

Potential energy:

U = ½kx²

U = ½(50)(0.18²)

U = 0.81 J

Because E = K + U,

K = 2.25 − 0.81

K = 1.44 J

Example 6 — Simple pendulum

Find the period of a 0.80 m simple pendulum near Earth's surface.

T = 2π√(L/g)

T = 2π√(0.80/9.81)

T ≈ 1.79 s

The mass of the bob is irrelevant in the ideal small-angle expression.

Example 7 — Determine phase from initial conditions

Suppose x₀ = 0 and the oscillator is moving in the positive x direction with maximum speed.

Using

x = A cos(ωt + φ)

at t = 0:

0 = A cos φ

Therefore φ may be ±π/2.

Velocity is

v = −ωA sin(ωt + φ)

We need v > 0. Therefore sin φ must be negative.

φ = −π/2

Example 8 — Compare two spring oscillators

Oscillator B has four times the mass of oscillator A but the same spring constant. How do their periods compare?

T = 2π√(m/k)

Therefore

TB / TA = √(mB/mA)

TB / TA = √4

TB = 2TA

Example 9 — Resonance concept

An oscillator has a natural angular frequency of 8 rad/s. Compare drivers at 2, 7.8 and 20 rad/s.

The 7.8 rad/s driver is closest to the natural frequency.

It will therefore generally produce the strongest steady-state response, assuming comparable driving force amplitudes and ordinary damping.

The exact resonance frequency and peak size depend on damping.
